# Design Pass 9 — Major Evolutionary Transitions and True Multicellular Individuality

## Central Principle

True multicellularity is a transition in evolutionary individuality, not a cell-count threshold.

## Evidence of Higher-Order Individuality

The strongest evidence combines:

- collective reproduction;
- developmental heredity;
- reproductive bottleneck;
- stable differentiation;
- internal transport and physiology;
- conflict suppression;
- fate alignment;
- dependence of cells on the whole.

No single metric is sufficient.

## Locked Decisions

- Cells remain active authoritative biological entities inside organisms.
- A collective that grows but does not reproduce as a collective is not a complete higher-order individual.
- Propagules may be single-cell, multicell, fragmentary, or chimeric.
- Narrow bottlenecks strongly support relatedness and reproducible development but are not universal requirements.
- The genome encodes local developmental rules, not an adult body blueprint.
- Development uses division orientation, adhesion, polarity, signals, contact, mechanics, timing, and state inheritance.
- Asymmetric division may produce daughter cells with different developmental states and materials.
- Morphogen-like gradients are physical fields with sources, diffusion, degradation, and receptor thresholds.
- Cell types and tissues are analytical patterns of stable function and regulation.
- Differentiation may be reversible, stable, or terminal.
- Germ-soma-like division is important but not mandatory for every path.
- Somatic sacrifice becomes stable through shared-genome transmission and conflict control.
- Cells may lose independent functions and become obligately dependent on the organism.
- Internal transport progresses from diffusion and contact transfer toward specialized channels and directional flow.
- Body axes, polarity, symmetry, structural support, modularity, and regeneration emerge developmentally.
- Regulated cell death may shape development, repair damage, create channels, and suppress conflict.
- Cancer-like growth emerges from ordinary somatic mutation and failed regulation.
- Somatic and heritable reproductive mutations are distinguished.
- Internal policing is local, costly, imperfect, and vulnerable to false positives.
- Organismal homeostasis is distributed rather than centrally assigned.
- Organism death may occur while some component cells survive.
- Aging and senescence are emergent tradeoffs, not mandatory timers.
- Large size and complexity retain transport, development, maintenance, and reproduction costs.
- New cell types emerge through regulatory duplication and divergence.
- Existing development constrains future evolution.
- Endosymbiotic, chimeric, network, branching, sessile, modular, and distributed individuals remain possible.
- Multicellularity classification is evidence-based, uncertain, and noncausal.
- Integration may reverse or simplify where dependency permits.

## Validation Scenarios

- repeatable local development;
- signal removal changes morphology through expected path;
- one genotype produces several cell states;
- propagule recreates organization;
- small mutation changes morphology;
- component-cell independent viability;
- collective reproductive success;
- bottleneck conflict comparison;
- reproductive specialization;
- cancer-like invasion;
- policing cost and error;
- size limit without transport;
- transport innovation;
- regeneration and overgrowth;
- organism death versus fragment survival.

## Deferred Decisions

Exact developmental-state encoding, morphogen system, cell-type clustering, germline representation, internal pumping, support mechanics, apoptosis analogue, immunity analogue, endosymbiosis, metamorphosis, and organism abstraction remain deferred.

## Acceptance Statement

A multicellular organism exists only when a heritable lifecycle repeatedly constructs and reproduces an integrated body whose cells’ evolutionary futures are substantially aligned with that body.
