# Design Pass 11 — Connected Regions, Ecological Geography, Migration, Isolation, and Biome-Like Worlds

## Central Principle

The world expands through physically connected habitats. Geography changes evolution through constrained movement, material transport, isolation, local conditions, and history.

## Locked Decisions

- Early expansion remains microscopic rather than immediately planetary.
- Each region has local continuous space, fields, organisms, geometry, fidelity state, and history.
- Regions form a large-scale graph connected by explicit physical routes.
- Connection properties include width, length, direction, flow, permeability, environmental filter, schedule, and open/closed state.
- Connections transport organisms, propagules, material, toxins, signals, dead biomass, heat, and flow where applicable.
- Organisms physically cross boundaries at full fidelity; no population percentage teleports.
- Crossing preserves identity, genotype, materials, damage, memory, symbionts, and lifecycle state.
- Body size and geometry may prevent passage.
- Passage may fragment a colony or organism.
- Dispersal traits, dormancy, hitchhiking, and partner co-dispersal may evolve.
- Arrival, survival, reproduction, establishment, and ecological integration are separate stages.
- Repeated arrival increases establishment only through actual propagule pressure.
- Community assembly depends on colonization order and environmental modification.
- Local adaptation and migration-selection balance arise from ordinary reproduction and gene flow.
- Isolation may be physical, chemical, temporal, behavioral, ecological, body-size-dependent, or partner-dependent.
- Regional branches retain ancestry, divergence, range, and later secondary-contact history.
- Endemic, widespread, specialist, generalist, relict, refuge, source, sink, stepping-stone, and rescue are analytical categories.
- Range fronts, contraction, contact zones, barrier zones, refugia, and moving boundaries are spatial outcomes.
- Regional disturbances propagate with finite delay and attenuation.
- Environmental cycles may drive repeated or lifecycle-specific migration.
- Biome-like and ecological-province labels are detected from actual conditions and communities.
- There are no biome bonuses or fixed Earth categories.
- Different regions may converge functionally or diverge under similar conditions.
- There is no global evolutionary age; biological complexity may be geographically uneven.
- Life may create or destroy routes through matrix, degradation, blockage, or flow redirection.
- Player geographic interventions are explicit and recorded.
- All regions share one coherent logical timeline.
- Distant regions do not freeze because the camera leaves.
- Large worlds may later use validated fidelity tiers, but current authoritative regional state must remain preserved.
- Rehydration cannot invent exact past positions or new adaptation.

## Validation Scenarios

- directional field transfer;
- organism crossing;
- multicellular passage constraint;
- fragmentation during passage;
- failed colonization;
- propagule-pressure establishment;
- priority effect;
- local adaptation;
- gene-flow effect;
- source-sink;
- route closure and reopening;
- secondary contact;
- disturbance propagation;
- local extinction and recolonization;
- regional save/load;
- fair simultaneous boundary exchange.

## Deferred Decisions

Exact region count, geometry, transfer equations, dormancy, speciation analysis, biome classification, world-map representation, background fidelity tiers, cohort models, rehydration, and planetary extension remain deferred.

## Acceptance Statement

Ecological geography is valid only when place matters because organisms and materials travel through real constrained routes and when regional labels remain interpretations rather than modifiers.
