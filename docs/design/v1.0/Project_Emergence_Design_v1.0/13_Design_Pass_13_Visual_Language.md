# Design Pass 13 — Visual Language, Organic Presentation, Scientific Overlays, and Scale Transitions

## Central Principle

Authoritative simulation geometry and visual interpretation are separate. Rendering may smooth and translate data but cannot invent biologically meaningful events.

## Locked Decisions

- Natural and scientific views interpret one authoritative state.
- The hidden field grid and spatial partitions are invisible in normal view.
- The habitat must not resemble graph paper, tiles, a spreadsheet, or a cellular-automaton board.
- Natural presentation is biologically evocative, materially grounded, restrained, and capable of alien form.
- Scientific presentation uses calibrated contours, arrows, labels, networks, numbers, and uncertainty.
- Natural organism color derives from expressed traits where practical and is separate from lineage-overlay color.
- Critical meaning never relies only on hue.
- Cells render as bounded living bodies with membrane, internal activity, deformation, growth, division, damage, and death.
- Unsupported familiar organelles are not added decoratively.
- Growth and division are gradual.
- Death leaves biomass and environmental consequence.
- Predation appears through real pursuit, contact, chemistry, damage, and transfer.
- Visuals distinguish active propulsion from drift.
- Sensing, memory, and regulation are subtle naturally and explicit in focused scientific views.
- Colonies look integrated through contact, bonds, matrix, transport, and shared deformation.
- Multicellular organisms visibly develop from propagules.
- Differentiated cells and tissues look different only when state or function differs.
- Internal transport, stress, regeneration, and cancer-like growth are visible when focused.
- The environment is active through geometry, flow, suspended material, light, temperature, and fields.
- Observation illumination is distinct from simulated biological light.
- Distant organisms transition into density, motion, and ecological textures rather than square heatmaps.
- Selected lineages remain trackable across scales without replacing natural appearance.
- Soft ecological transitions are used unless physical boundaries are hard.
- Semantic zoom changes representation by scale.
- Rendering detail and simulation fidelity are separate.
- Camera focus preserves ecological context.
- Selection styling is distinct from biological glow.
- Labels use semantic disclosure and screen-space readability.
- Events are restrained and non-gamified.
- Historical ghosting and replay reveal whether data is exact, reconstructed, or approximate.
- Overlays declare data source, variable, scale, time window, normalization, and uncertainty.
- Categorical views emphasize selected categories rather than assigning unlimited bright colors.
- Solid and dashed relationships distinguish observation from inference.
- Numbers remain primarily in inspectors.
- High-speed rendering uses disclosed temporal aggregation.
- Particles may sample fields rather than represent molecules one-to-one.
- Audio is interpretive and optional.
- Accessibility is foundational.
- Visual presets alter presentation only.
- Procedural appearance is deterministic and stable across save/load.
- Convergence, mimicry, camouflage, and cryptic differences remain visually possible.
- Organism-perception views translate the world through actual evolved sensors.
- Visual primitives correspond to approved simulated biological capabilities.
- Evolved bodies must not resemble parts pasted together in a creature creator.
- Rendering scales through batching, instancing, culling, LOD, aggregate textures, and representative particles.
- Rendering frame rate never affects biology.
- Rehydration does not pretend to know discarded historical trajectories.
- Visual QA tests quantitative overlay accuracy and biological honesty.

## Acceptance Statement

The visual identity is an alien living ecology observed through a polished scientific instrument: organic at first glance, rigorous under inspection, and never visibly reduced to its implementation grid.
