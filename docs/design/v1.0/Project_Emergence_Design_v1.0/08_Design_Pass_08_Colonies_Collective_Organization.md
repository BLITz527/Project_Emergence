# Design Pass 8 — Colonies and Collective Organization

## Central Principle

Collective organization exists on a continuum. Proximity or adhesion alone does not create a new organism.

## Organizational Continuum

```text
temporary aggregation
→ repeated association
→ adhesive cluster
→ persistent colony
→ coordinated collective
→ integrated higher-order individual
```

No stage is guaranteed or irreversible.

## Locked Decisions

- Adhesion is a physical capability using compatible surfaces, receptors, or matrix.
- Bonds possess strength, elasticity, rest length, maintenance cost, and break conditions.
- Cells may regulate attachment and detachment.
- Extracellular matrix is real produced material with persistence, degradation, structural effects, and public-good vulnerability.
- Colony morphology emerges from division geometry, adhesion, matrix, mechanics, flow, and local growth.
- There is no fixed morphology catalogue or colony shape blueprint.
- Crowding and pressure create real internal conditions.
- Interior and exterior differences arise physically.
- Cells infer position from contact, fields, pressure, resources, and signals rather than global colony coordinates.
- Genetically identical cells may specialize through regulation.
- Clonal and chimeric collectives are both possible.
- Fusion and rejection use real recognition and adhesion.
- Resource sharing uses environmental or contact transfer; there is no shared colony inventory.
- Larger size creates transport, waste, drag, signaling, structural, and conflict costs.
- Coordination remains local and decentralized.
- Collective movement results from member forces and bond structure.
- Collective feeding, predation, and defense emerge from member behavior and morphology.
- Cell-level reproductive conflict remains active.
- Cheaters may reduce matrix, oversupply reproduction, withhold transfer, manipulate signals, or escape dangerous roles.
- Higher-level selection arises only when collectives produce descendant collectives.
- Colonies may reproduce by fragmentation, budding, single-cell propagule, multicell propagule, or chimeric propagule.
- Heritable collective structure is reconstructed developmentally.
- Collective identity is mainly analytical and historical; it cannot command members.
- Individuality is assessed across persistence, boundary, exchange, coordination, differentiation, reproductive unity, bottleneck, conflict suppression, and fate alignment.
- Colony death, cell death, and lineage extinction are distinct.
- Colonies may host their own associated ecological communities.
- Facultative and obligate coloniality are both valid.
- Integration may strengthen, collapse, or reverse.

## Validation Scenarios

- bond formation and breakage;
- division while attached;
- emergent morphology;
- interior gradient;
- identical-genotype specialization;
- real resource sharing;
- cheater invasion;
- bottleneck conflict reduction;
- fragment reproduction;
- collective movement;
- colony lifecycle;
- split/merge ancestry;
- conservation and save/load.

## Deferred Decisions

Exact bond physics, matrix representation, deformation, contact transfer, internal transport, colony identity algorithm, individuality metric, policing, organism abstraction, and maximum explicit size remain deferred.

## Acceptance Statement

A colony is valid only when its persistence, exchange, morphology, lifecycle, and conflict arise from cells, bonds, fields, and regulation rather than a colony flag or central controller.
